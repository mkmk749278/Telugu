# Be Practical with Kishore Telugu: playbook

`CLAUDE.md` holds the rules every session follows. This file holds how each stage is done and the reason behind every
rule. New lessons go into the section they belong to, with the reason and the date; never a new instruction file.
Research reports live in `docs/research/` and are evidence, not instructions.

**Reference repos (read, never edit):** `mkmk749278/Creator` (the English channel: the cinematic standard, narration
craft, picture rules, tools) and `mkmk749278/Margin-of-Error` (the forensic standard: findings attributed to the
investigator, fairness, exhibits). When this file is silent on craft, Creator's `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` is the standard;
when it is silent on fairness, Margin of Error's is.

## 1. The channel (owner, 2026-11-13)

**What it is:** forensic documentaries in spoken Telugu about the big physical projects of Andhra Pradesh and
Telangana: why a barrage sank, why a tunnel collapsed, how a hillside stores a city's evening power. One mechanism,
shown in 3D, proved with the investigators' own pages, told with no party and no fluff (సోది లేకుండా).

**The promise to the viewer:** "You saw the headline. Here is what actually happened under the ground, and what the
official report says, explained so your whole family understands it."

**The edge in one sentence:** the first Telugu channel that explains why a Telugu project failed or works by showing
the mechanism in 3D and the investigators' own page on screen, with no party and no shouting
(`docs/research/2026-11-13_content_edge.md`).

**Two tracks**
- **Track B, the lead: regional infrastructure autopsies.** Dams and barrages, tunnels, lift irrigation, power and
  storage, metros, ports, industrial accidents in the two states.
- **Track C, second: corporate and financial autopsies** with a Telugu address (Karvy, AgriGold, Satyam, a real-estate
  pre-launch collapse). It enters first as a chapter inside Track B films (project finance, benefit-cost ratio,
  contractor insolvency); separate Track C films start after the three pilots.

**What we do not make** (the audit's scores, and our own result):
- 20th-century political turning points and political biographies. *Why:* party legacies, comment wars, archive footage
  we do not own. The Sonia film (Nov 2026) was this kind; it has 3 views two days after release.
- Geopolitics and intelligence stories. *Why:* the world-news films are the weakest on every Telugu explainer channel
  we measured, and the subject has no mechanism to draw.
- Daily news, trivia lists, stock tips, motivation. *Why:* the big channels own these with volume we will not match.

**Why this direction (measured 13 Nov 2026):** channels of 1,400 and 15,000 subscribers reached 1.4 lakh and 14 lakh
views on a named local project with a rupee figure and a "what went wrong" question, about 110 times their normal
film. YouTube carries this subject to viewers whatever the channel's size. The "complete story" and "status update"
films exist; the failure explained by its mechanism from the official report does not.

## 2. The audience

- **Who:** educated Telugu viewers of 22–45 in the cities of both states and abroad (the US first: Texas, New Jersey,
  Virginia, California, Illinois, Georgia), and their families on the living-room TV. They now leave Telugu for
  English and Hindi channels when they want calm analysis.
- **Where they watch:** the TV first. Andhra Pradesh has about 10.10 million reachable connected-TV viewers; rural
  Telangana has the highest penetration in the dataset (32.2%). *Rule:* every frame reads from across a room; the
  number that matters is said, not only shown; nothing in the film needs a pause to read.
- **What they are tired of:** shouting debates, party framing, "top 10 facts", thumbnails the video contradicts, long
  intros ("video starts at 2:32").
- **What they reward** (from the most-liked comments on the Telugu leaders): the patient "tuition master" who explains
  what they did not understand, sincerity, their own money and their own town taken seriously.
- **Both states, equally.** Alternate the state from film to film where the slate allows; "మన తెలంగాణ, మన ఆంధ్ర";
  never one region's Telugu as "pure" or another's as a joke.
- **Respect first:** tell every death with dignity (the eight men in the SLBC tunnel are people with names and
  families, never a count); no victim images; suicide by Press Council norms with a helpline.

## 3. The film formula

**One project the viewer already knows + one rupee figure + one physical mechanism + one official document.**

- **The mechanism is the hero.** Piping under a raft, a shear zone flushing silt, water lifted uphill at noon. If the
  mechanism cannot be drawn in one cutaway, the topic is not ready.
- **The document is the proof.** NDSA, CAG, CWC, the High Court, SEBI, the project's own compliance filing. The page
  appears on screen as the real scan with the line highlighted and its date and report number.
- **The money is the stake**, in rupees with Indian grouping and brought to a household scale (per family, per acre,
  per unit of power).
- **Physics, not parties.** We audit soil, water and concrete. Every government that touched the project is named
  with its dates in the same tone; no party colours, symbols or slogans; the film never says who to vote for or blame.
- **Zero sodhi:** no greeting, no channel intro, no "in this video", no recap of what was just said.

**Structure** (Creator's, kept):
- Cold open inside the real moment (0:00–0:08), first frame = the thumbnail's subject, **sentence 1 confirms the
  thumbnail**; the paradox by 0:25; an open loop by 0:45.
- Chapters of 2–4 minutes, each with a question, a turn and a payoff. A usual order: the moment → how it was meant to
  work → what was different here → the mechanism step by step (the set piece) → what the report found and what the
  other side says → the money → where it stands today and the next date to watch.
- **The film ends in today's facts.** Projects move (the SLBC tunnel crossed its shear zone in October 2026): the last
  chapter is re-checked within 24 hours of upload.
- **The ending:** one saying to take home, the yardstick and date to watch, the subscribe line, one real question for
  the comments. Channel name only here.
- **Length:** the story decides; 12–18 minutes is where this subject's winners sit (measured: 9–33 minutes, medians
  10–17).

### 4.3 The topic test (before any brief)
A topic goes ahead only with a yes to all six:
1. Does an ordinary viewer in either state know the project's name?
2. Is there a rupee figure from an audit, budget or filing?
3. Is there one physical mechanism we can draw in a cutaway?
4. Is there a primary document we can read ourselves (not only news about it)?
5. Is there a question people actually ask? Run `scripts/yt_topic_scan.py` with the Telugu question and the English
   name: views on clips and Shorts show the demand; long films of 8 minutes and over show the supply.
6. What can we show that the best existing film does not? Write it as our edge in one sentence. No edge, no film.

*Why the scan:* low competition can also mean low demand (Pinnapuram's name returns nothing above 40,000 views), and
a crowded topic needs a new angle (Hyderabad Metro).

## 5. Facts and fairness

- **Facts only from collected sources**; every factual line has a row in `projects/<slug>/claims.csv` (claim, URL,
  verbatim quote, status). Knowledge stops at June 2026; a research report from any chatbot (the niche audit
  included) gives leads for the film's facts: each figure is found again in its primary source before it is narrated.
  *Why even for a trusted report:* the audit's own SLBC ending was already out of date in October 2026.
- **Primary first:** NDSA and CWC reports, CAG audit reports (cag.gov.in, many in Telugu too), court orders,
  commission reports, CEA lists, SEBI orders, environmental compliance filings, tender and contract documents. News is
  for dates and for what each side said.
- **Attribute every finding:** "NDSA నివేదిక ప్రకారం", "కాగ్ లెక్క ప్రకారం". Our own reading is said as ours
  ("OUR CALCULATION" on screen).
- **The other side, always:** the government of the time, the present government, the contractor and the engineers
  each get their published answer. A disputed finding is said as disputed.
- **Real companies and people** (L&T, Megha, JP Associates, Greenko, officials): only in their public role and only
  what the record shows; a pending case is pending; no guilt without a judgment.
- **No false absolutes:** "first", "largest", "never", "impossible" are claims and need a source.
- **Fresh-facts gate:** rows that can change (status, cost, dates, court orders) are searched again within 24 hours of
  upload.
- **The words:** dialect-neutral; never చండాలం, never కటిక చీకటి (use కారు చీకటి); no caste names as adjectives; no
  proverb that insults disability or gender (the Oct 2026 report, rules 14–15).
- **Money films (Track C):** education only; no buy, sell or hold on any named security; "Educational only, not
  SEBI-registered" on screen and in the description.

## 6. Narration

- **Spoken Telugu with natural Tenglish**, an elder brother explaining over chai (Creator archive
  `docs/archive/PLAYBOOK_2026-11-05.md` §4b for the full craft). Never నువ్వు to the viewer; no newspaper connectors;
  no grandhika endings.
- **Engineering terms stay in English** inside Telugu sentences, the way engineers in Hyderabad say them: diaphragm
  wall, secant pile, shear zone, benefit-cost ratio, tunnel boring machine, pumped storage. **Each term is explained
  once with a picture from the viewer's own home** (piping: water finding its way under a bund in the field; uplift:
  an empty steel pot pushed down into a full bucket), then used freely.
- **Simple is never childish.** No physics lecture: the term arrives only when the story needs it.
- One or two "మీకో డౌట్ రావచ్చు" moments per film: the viewer's own objection, answered.
- Numbers at human scale; one number per sentence; lakh and crore.
- Sentence 1 confirms the thumbnail; the opening (0:00–0:45) is written and checked first; the owner approves the
  narration before any audio.
- **Recording text** (`vo.txt`): Telugu script only, English words written in Telugu script, numbers as English number
  words in Telugu script (టూ థౌజండ్ ఫోర్), no digits, Latin letters, `...`, `!`, quotes, brackets or tags; questions
  end with `?`. Add every new engineering term to a pronunciation list in the film folder before the blocks go out.

## 7. Audio

- **The owner generates the audio himself. Never call any TTS API from a session.** Blocks of **at most 10,000
  characters** (owner, 2026-11-13: Eleven v4 takes 10,000 characters; this replaces the 1,500 limit of 2026-11-10),
  split at paragraphs by `scripts/vo_blocks.py` (default `--max 10000`), in the chat as click-to-copy blocks and in
  `projects/<slug>/audio_blocks.md`. Every take is checked against the script for skipped lines before it is used.
- **Voice engine: open, the owner's decision.** The audit asks for an A/B before the pilot voice is locked: the same
  two technical Tenglish paragraphs on the current ElevenLabs voice and on Sarvam Bulbul v3, judged by ear for
  engineering words, numbers and names. When the owner asks, the session prepares the two test paragraphs; the owner
  generates both and chooses.
- **Joining and timing:** `scripts/vo_rejoin.py` joins the takes (room tone in the gaps, a fade-in on each take, no
  word touched); `scripts/vo_timing.py` matches takes to paragraphs by length, places every sentence on the take's
  own pauses (`scripts/align_script.py`) and writes `vo/timing.json`. Timing never comes from speech recognition.
- **The take check** (Medigadda, 14 Nov 2026): pace of each take and each sentence first; then a Whisper transcript
  in windows of at most 6.5 s, only to confirm every sentence and number is there. *Why short windows:* on whole
  takes the model dropped half the Telugu text and looked like missing audio; and it garbles English year words
  ("టూ థౌజండ్ ట్వెంటీ …"), so a low match on a year is re-read in its own window before anyone is asked for a new take.

## 8. Picture

- **Built for the TV:** 1920×1080 authored, 4K master; labels of 2–4 words; ≥ 44 px for what the voice points to and
  ≥ 30 px for other facts at 1080; 7:1 contrast; Telugu on-screen text in a tested Telugu font; ₹ with Indian
  grouping, dates day-first.
- **The hero cutaway** (Blender Cycles, 2–4 per film): the structure and the ground under it, on a physical base (a
  drafting table, a terrain block from real elevation data), never an object in a void. Designed from real drawings
  and photos first; labelled ILLUSTRATION; proportions that are not from a drawing say so.
- **The document walk:** the real page (NDSA, CAG, court order) in one desk pose, pulling from the letterhead to the
  highlighted line, with the report's name, number and date. Never retyped as if it were the original.
  **Read the whole report for pictures, not only text** (2026-11-14): the NDSA committee report of 2023 had its own
  site photos (the guarded deck, the structure at the water line) and the crack map of pier 20, which ended "type on
  paper" in two chapters and confirmed a number (1.256 m) that we had only from the news. Shown as the report's page,
  with its name and year. Keep a highlight clear of the captions (`DocPage lift`).
- **The camera never leaves its plate** (2026-11-14): a keyframe near a plate's edge showed a black band (s26, s141);
  the shared `Plate` now clamps zoom and centre, so no keyframe can do it again. Check a plate family's cameras at full
  resolution before building on low-resolution stand-ins: the measured anchors of `h3` moved up to 9% of the frame.
- **Sound on every visual event, cued from the code** (2026-11-14): `tools/sfx_cues.py` reads each document, card,
  ring, pile and pin from the chapters and `tools/sfx.py` synthesises the sound on its frame (nothing to license);
  re-run both after any picture change, then the mix. Measured 13–18 dB under the voice; the owner's ears are the check.
- **The place:** satellite and terrain plates, India's official boundary on every map, the town and district named.
- **Real people at work in every chapter:** licensed photos and footage of the site, engineers, workers, farmers
  (Commons, GODL-India, PIB, agency releases with a licence, the owner's Cowork fetches). Every asset in
  `media_manifest.csv` with its licence; commercial-use licences only.
- **No TV-news or film clips.** *Why:* Section 52 fair dealing is narrow, and such clips are the main takedown risk
  on Telugu YouTube. Our own 3D and graphics plus short quoted excerpts of public reports keep the risk low; they do
  not remove the need for a licence on every photo.
- **Word-by-word sync** as in Creator: every name, number, place and key verb gets its picture on its word; a new
  shot about every 3.5–5 s; no static plate over 1.5 s; no run of diagrams over about 20 s without a real picture.
- **Each film its own look** (palette, base, motif) inside the brand. *Why:* a catalogue that looks templated is a
  monetisation risk (YouTube's inauthentic-content rule).
- **AI disclosure:** "Altered content" = No for cutaways, data graphics and a synthetic narrator of no real person;
  Yes if a film ever shows a realistic made-up scene of a real event or place. Labels on screen always
  (ILLUSTRATION, ILLUSTRATIVE FOOTAGE, OUR CALCULATION).

## 9. Package

- **Locked in the plan, before the script:** the promise in one sentence, the title with two alternatives that keep
  the same promise, the thumbnail concept.
- **Title:** the project's name + the rupee figure or the place + the question, in Telugu with the English name
  people type (Medigadda, SLBC, Polavaram). No channel suffix. *Evidence:* the breakouts were "₹6000Cr నష్టం | తప్పు
  ఎక్కడ జరిగింది?" and "Hyderabadలో నీళ్లు లేవు? అసలు Problem ఏంటి?".
- **Thumbnail:** one hero cutaway with the failing part glowing, at most 3 Telugu words, bright enough for a TV
  across the room, bottom-right corner empty; a main and two challengers that vary picture and words, never the
  promise. No politician's face, no party colour.
- **Description:** 2–4 plain lines with the search phrases in Telugu and English, chapters from `00:00`; sources in the
  pinned comment. Telugu SRT, and an English SRT for the diaspora's children and for search.
- **The owner sees the title and thumbnails in the chat and chooses before anything goes public.**
  `scripts/yt_upload.py` uploads private; `--only publish` runs only after that choice.

## 10. Growth and money

- **Goal until monetisation: subscribers and watch hours** (1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in 12
  months; reports say 8,000 hours for applications from 1 Feb 2027: check YouTube's own page before planning on it).
- **Cadence (owner, 2026-11-13): two films a week**, the state alternating where the slate allows. Never two films on
  one project inside a fortnight. Research runs ahead of the films in production; the fact and fairness gates and the
  owner's approvals stay as they are.
- **Shorts (owner, 2026-11-13): never a cut of the film; each Short has its own script**, the way Margin of Error
  makes them (its PLAYBOOK §13.5, §13.5a). The form: "గుర్తుందా? మీరు విన్నది ఒకటి, రిపోర్ట్ చెప్పింది ఇంకొకటి":
  set up the case, show the report's page, **keep back the film's answer**, end on the open question and the full
  film. About 25–45 s, vertical, built from the film's cutaways and documents with its own narration (the owner
  approves the script and generates the audio), frame 0 as the cover, clear of the Shorts UI, linked to the film.
  Every spoken fact has a `claims.csv` row. *Why:* Shorts on these exact subjects reached 15 to 66 lakh views; they
  pay almost nothing but are how a channel with one subscriber is found.
- **Revenue:** the audit's blended RPM (₹210–313 per 1,000 views with 10–15% US viewers) is a calculator estimate.
  Plan from the real RPM and the real US share in Studio once the channel has them.
- **After every film** (24 hours, 7 days, 28 days): impressions and CTR, still watching at 0:30, average view
  duration, traffic sources, the US share, subscribers per 1,000 views; every comment read into the next film. The
  session's token cannot read Analytics: the owner sends Studio screenshots, or adds a token with
  `yt-analytics.readonly`.
- **Baseline, 13 Nov 2026:** 2 videos, 1 subscriber, 14 views.

## 11. How a film is made

The order is Creator's ("Promise first", "decide early, build once"); owner gates in **bold**.
1. **Topic** through the topic test (§4.3).
2. **Research** → `research.md`: what everyone says vs what the documents say, our edge in one sentence, what real
   pictures exist, the story bank (15+ sourced moments, people, objects, numbers that can become things), the
   objection ledger from the top comments on existing films, draft `claims.csv`.
3. **Plan** → `plan.md`: angles, the locked promise (title + thumbnail concept), cold opens, endings, the beat sheet
   (each beat's question, turn, picture, document, sound), the hero cutaways, fairness flags. **The owner approves
   the plan with its title and thumbnail concept.**
4. **Facts** checked at `max` effort before the narration is final.
5. **Narration**: the opening first, then the rest; **the owner approves the narration.** In parallel from plan
   approval: document and photo requests (one click-to-copy Cowork request; YouTube clips are not used) and the
   Blender heroes.
6. **Audio** by the owner from the blocks; checked; aligned.
7. **Word map and picture** built to the locked audio.
8. **Render on GitHub Actions** (`remotion-render.yml`), at most 6 runners at once.
9. **Music and sound** from Drive `Music-Library/` (`scripts/music_pull.sh`).
10. **720p review cut** after our own full review; **the owner approves the cut.**
11. **4K master**, then the package; **the owner chooses the title and thumbnail**; upload private, publish on the
    owner's word; pin the source comment; the fresh-facts re-check.

### 11.1 Independent reviews (owner, 2026-11-13; method from Margin of Error §14.5)
Three reviews per film, each in **a new cloud session with no repository attached** (so it judges as a stranger, not
against our rules), archived when done. Prompt: `prompts/audience_review_v1.md`.
- **At the plan:** the edge sentence, the beat sheet and the strongest existing films. Would these viewers click and
  stay? Which of their questions are missing? → `review/plan_review.md`.
- **At the narration:** the full text; a lean-in map, explain-back, fairness, and the comparison with existing films
  → `review/audience_vN.md`. Before the owner sees the narration.
- **At the 720p cut:** did it give viewers what they came for, better than the others? → `review/viewer_cut_rN.md`.

**The panel is chosen for the film, never reused blindly.** Seven viewers: the people who know the subject best, the
people it touches, and the people most likely to object. Always include: (1) a working or retired engineer of that
exact field; (2) someone whose life the project touches (the farmer in the command area, the worker, the resident);
(3) a supporter of each party that governed the project, one each, listening for bias; (4) a lawyer or senior
journalist listening for anything unfair to a named company or person; (5) a young professional who leaves at the
first padding; (6) an elder watching on the family TV who needs simple Telugu; (7) a Telugu viewer abroad. Both
states are on every panel. For Medigadda: a retired irrigation chief engineer from Warangal; a paddy farmer from
Peddapalli; a BRS supporter from Siddipet and a Congress supporter from Nalgonda; a High Court advocate in Hyderabad;
a software engineer in Gachibowli; a retired teacher in Vijayawada; a civil engineer in Dallas.
Every review is a list of leads: each point is checked and answered in `review/<name>_check.md` (CONFIRMED / PARTLY /
NOT CONFIRMED, then FIXED / DECLINED with the reason); a reviewer's rewrite never adds a fact.

**Polished only:** the owner never receives a draft, test or placeholder.
**Drive:** `Creator/Telugu/<slug>/`, one folder per film; channel-level files in `Creator/Telugu/_Channel/`.

## 12. Lessons log (newest last; each with its reason)
- **2026-11-13, Medigadda narration v1.** An independent panel of eight (engineer, farmer, a supporter of each party,
  advocate, young professional, elder, viewer abroad) found what our own reading missed. Rules that came out of it:
  - *A reconstruction is never spoken as witnessed.* Start it with "రిపోర్ట్ ల ప్రకారం" and say nobody saw below the
    ground. Why: the same film said the evidence was destroyed by grouting.
  - *Never tease what a source did not do.* "The engineers looked below" is a claim; check it against the other
    side's reply.
  - *Answer each point of the other side one by one, or say it was not found.* One reply to three points reads as
    closing the case, and the party's supporter leaves.
  - *Name every government with its dates; do not use verbs with no subject* ("దింపారు", "తీసేశారు"), unless the
    record does not say who, and then say that.
  - *A court is not a "side".* State the operative order narrowly; read the order itself.
  - *Never print a "likely to exceed" figure as a fact in a title.*
  - *Do not name a junior official more than once, if at all.*
  - *English number words lose elders and farmers* (open with the owner; recommended: Telugu words for counts and
    money, English for years).
  - *Speaking rate:* about 15 Telugu characters a second (Sonia film: 11,407 characters ran 12:37). A 16,500-character
    narration is about 18 minutes.
  - *When the primary document is not online, say so in the narration* and put its reading on the pre-publish
    checklist; every claim from news about it is marked "secondary" in `claims.csv`.
- **2026-11-14, Medigadda audio and picture.**
  - *Ask Cowork for the documents before the narration goes to audio, not after.* The committee's own letter
    arrived after the takes were made and showed one wrong title (a member called the chairman): a take had to be
    made again. Why: a news report's description of who someone is can be loose; the letterhead is not.
  - *Hero shots as plates, motion in Remotion.* The cutaway is rendered in Blender as stills of each state from a
    few fixed cameras, with the named points of the model projected into `anchors.json`; Remotion dissolves between
    states, moves the camera and draws water, sand and labels on those points. Why: 18 minutes of animated Cycles is
    days of rendering; forty plates are an hour, and every overlay still lands on the model.
  - *Labels pinned to a plate keep their screen size* (they divide by the camera zoom). Why: at 2x zoom a 46 px
    label became 92 px and ran off the frame.
  - *A cut is the next shot fading in over the last one, which stays underneath for the fade.* Why: ending a shot
    on the frame the next begins dipped every cut to black.
  - *Check every word cue before rendering* (`scripts/cue_check.py`). Why: one cue on a word that is not in its
    sentence stops a whole render shard.
